# Evidence

> Raw signals — quotes, observations, competitive proof, market data. **This is where you start.**

**Stage:** `research`  ·  **OS surface:** /app/races/research/evidence

> ▶ **Build order: `01_evidence/` is populated first — before the charter, before positioning, before anything.** The chain of command runs upward: evidence → patterns → insights → articulation, and the charter itself is *derived* from what you collect here (see [`00_charter/`](../00_charter/README.md)). Author strategy before evidence and you'll produce a brand nobody can defend when challenged.

## How to populate this folder

The **research-pass prompt** does this collection for you: it gathers customer quotes, competitive entries, and market signals — roughly **30–50 source-attributed rows**, each linking back to where it came from — and writes them straight into these sub-folders.

- **research-pass prompt** — linked from the pre-os quickstart ([`00_START_HERE.md`](../00_START_HERE.md)) and the [starter-kit page](https://brandhorse.com/resources/brand-operating-system-starter-kit). It's **view-only on purpose** — make your own copy, fill in only the phase-0 scope questions, and run it in a named project with this scaffold loaded.

> **Pre-revenue? Expect little or no `internal-proof/` on your first pass — that's correct, not a gap.** Internal proof (shipped case studies, retention data, sales-call patterns) accumulates as you operate. Gather any call recordings, demos, or customer conversations you already have; that's what closes it fastest.

## What goes here

- `customer-quotes/` — Verbatims from interviews, calls, support tickets, reviews.
- `competitive/` — Competitor moves, positioning shifts, screenshots, ad libraries.
- `market-signals/` — Industry data, trend reports, search/SERP changes.
- `internal-proof/` — Sales calls, case-study source material, retention data.

## What doesn't go here

If you're tempted to put something in this folder that doesn't fit any of the
subfolders above, ask whether the artifact actually belongs upstream or
downstream. Most "where does this go?" moments come from skipping a step in
the chain of command.
